Measuring Latency

Percentiles, Distributions & Monitoring — A Visual Guide

1 — Why Average Latency Is Dangerous

Many teams use average latency to measure system performance. This is a mistake. The average hides important problems.

Example: 10 Requests

❌ What Average Shows

56.1 ms

"The system is OK." — Wrong.

The average mixes 9 fast requests with 1 very slow request. The result looks normal. But 1 in 10 users waited 450 ms.

✅ What Percentiles Show

P5012.5 msTypical user
P9054 msSlow users
P99450 msWorst case

Now you can see the outlier clearly.

2 — What Is a Percentile?

A percentile tells you: "X% of all requests are faster than this value."

PercentileMeaningFrequency of "worse"Typical Use
P50 (median)Half of the requests are faster1 in 2Typical user experience
P9090% of the requests are faster1 in 10General quality check
P9595% of the requests are faster1 in 20SLA target, broad alert
P9999% of the requests are faster1 in 100Tail latency, early warning
P99.999.9% of the requests are faster1 in 1,000Critical systems (payments)

4 — Compare: Same Average, Different Reality

Two services have almost the same average. But their percentiles show very different behavior.

Service A — Stable

All requests take approximately the same time.

Service B — Long Tail

⚠ Lesson: Average ≈ 50ms for both. But Service B's P95 is 7× worse. If you only monitor averages, you will not see this problem.

5 — The Fan-out Amplification Problem

In microservice systems, one user request often calls multiple backend services. The probability that all services respond quickly decreases with each added service.

6 — P95 vs P99: Different Detection Roles

P95 and P99 differ by only 4 percentage points. But they detect different types of problems.

Three Scenarios

🗑️
Scenario A: GC Pause — affects ~1% of requests
P9515ms ✅ Normal
P99500ms 🚨 Detected!

Only P99 can see this. The issue affects fewer than 5% of requests.

🗄️
Scenario B: DB Pool Exhaustion — affects ~10% of requests
P95300ms 🔔 Detected!
P99300ms 🚨 Also detected

Both detect this. The issue affects more than 5%.

📈
Scenario C: Gradual Memory Leak — gets worse over time
TimeP95P99
Hour 180ms150ms
Hour 285ms180ms
Hour 395ms250ms ⚠
Hour 4130ms ⚠500ms 🚨
Hour 5300ms 🔔2000ms 🔥

P99 detects 1–2 hours earlier. This gives you time to fix the problem before it affects many users.

P95 — The Heat Sensor 🌡️

  • Detects widespread problems
  • Stable signal, few false alarms
  • "Is the problem affecting many users?"
  • When P95 alerts → act immediately

P99 — The Smoke Detector 💨

  • Detects problems early
  • More sensitive, may have noise
  • "Is any user having a bad experience?"
  • When P99 alerts → investigate

7 — Monitoring: Which Metric Answers Which Question?

QuestionUse This
"Do most users have a good experience?"P50 or P75
"Is a degradation starting?"P99 — most sensitive
"Has the problem spread to many users?"P95
"What is the worst-case latency?"P99.9 or Max
"Is our SLA at risk?"The metric in the SLA (usually P95 or P99)
"What is the overall capacity?"Average — only for capacity math, not for alerting
💡 Rule: Never use average for alerting. Use it only for capacity planning with Little's Law (L = λ × W). For quality and alerting, always use percentiles.

8 — Alert Strategy

Summary: P99 is the smoke detector — it gives you an early warning. P95 is the heat sensor — when it triggers, the fire has already spread. You need both for effective monitoring. And never rely on averages alone.
